     "This section is my synthesis, not the lecturer's claims. Moerdijk is the 'more claims than land' argument of Witte et al. (2025) at village scale. And its multi-actor facet explains the 'who decides' question: the port authority, the municipality, the province and the national government all have stakes, and lecture 4's instruments (zoning, pre-emption, expropriation, project decisions by higher governments) are the tools that could turn fight, freeze or flight into policy.",
     "In Campbell's terms (lecture 3), Moerdijk sits on the property and development conflicts: port growth versus residents' homes, and energy transition versus local equity. Compensation planning resembles his idea of negotiated trade-offs, while Næss (lecture 1) would ask whether a sustainability goal justifies overriding a minority, recalling his warning about 'majority tyranny'. De Satgé and Watson (lecture 6) offer another lens: the port's techno-economic rationality and villagers' attachment to place can be read as conflicting rationalities meeting at an 'interface'. On the course dimensions, the case is situated and pluralist. The typology also shows that the outcome is a governance choice even when it looks like the natural result of port growth: freeze in particular reads as the result of indecision rather than a formal decision, so no one decides that the village should go, yet it slowly does. That makes it a clear example of an emergent rather than deliberately planned outcome."
