    "The first and best-known idea is the 'property contradiction'. Urban land and the built environment have a social character: their value and usefulness depend on collective investments such as streets, sewers and transit, on what happens on neighbouring plots, and on the needs of the economy as a whole, for example housing workers and moving goods. Yet land is privately owned and controlled, and each owner pursues their own return. The result is congestion, incompatible land uses, poor housing and threats to property values that no single owner can solve.",
    "Capital as a whole therefore needs some socialisation of control over land, through public works, zoning and planning. But the same socialisation threatens the rights and profits of individual owners and developers, who resist it. Planning is thus both necessary for capitalism and constrained by it: it can coordinate land use only as far as it does not undermine private property itself."
