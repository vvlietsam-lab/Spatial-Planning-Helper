    "The authors start from claims by Beck, Giddens and others that globalization and the end of Keynesian welfare systems have shifted risk from collective, state-led provision to 'individualisation', where people must take more responsibility for their own welfare (p. 2188).     "Advanced liberalism, in Dean's summary, includes marketized welfare, audit and consumerism, fragmented collective welfare, a reinvented notion of 'community', and strong discipline for deviant performance (p. 2190, note 2).     "The SRB, established in April 1994, was the most important English urban policy initiative since the mid-1990s (p. 2191). They keep in mind Foucault's warning that the dilemma of modern government is that 'if one governs too much one finishes by not being able to govern at all' (p. 2192).     "Policy documents of the time present communities as both subjects and objects of policy (pp. 2192–2193). The Commission for Social Justice wanted to build local capacities so people could shape their own futures; ministers spoke of giving communities 'responsibility', 'skills and confidence' and 'power'.     "Behind the moral language of participatory democracy lies what the authors call 'directed self-government' (p. 2194). Following Rose, they speak of 'communities of obligation': communities get more rights to define agendas but must accept wider responsibilities for implementing them.     "Bids also had to fit national frameworks and 'add value' to other programmes, which places responsibility for policy coherence on recipients rather than policymakers (p. 2195). The result is an 'empowerment' that brings no quantitative increase in power for communities but 'a qualitative transformation of forms of subjectivity to make government work' (p. 2195). Conditionality dominates: communities must accept centrally defined parameters and act 'responsibly', which limits the emancipatory potential while the state's capacity to shape conduct grows (p. 2196)."

Community knowledge is formally privileged, yet communities are seen as lacking technical skill, so representatives are trained by professionals to implement government objectives and monitoring.
